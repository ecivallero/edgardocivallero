1A place to begin

Welcome. My name is Edgardo Civallero. I am a librarian, archivist, writer, researcher, musician, and naturalist. My work connects knowledge systems, cultural memory, the musical instruments of Latin America, and ecology. I write from Quisquiza, among the mist, cloud forest, and páramo of Cundinamarca, Colombia, where I also care for and restore native Andean forest.

For more than twenty-five years, I have written books, essays, chronicles, and scholarly work; designed semantic and documentary infrastructures; and worked with libraries, archives, museums, scientific collections, and communities across Latin America. My projects bring together classification, memory, sound, territory, and living systems, with particular attention to forms of knowledge that conventional institutions fragment, silence, or fail to recognize.

25+ years across libraries, archives, and knowledge systems · Former head of the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute Library and Archive · Author and researcher with extensive work across Latin America · Based in Quisquiza, in the Colombian Andes

3Last Threads

From Quiquiza | Time

Published October 16, 2026

The latest post in my Critical notes series, "From Quisquiza | Time," explores how measuring growth over arbitrary periods can produce misleading conclusions. Drawing on tree growth in Quisquiza, it examines how funding cycles and performance indicators shape research and archival work, often overlooking important processes that develop slowly or remain difficult to quantify.


4Chronicles of Things Made

A log of recent writings, sounds, and gestures

Published October 18, 2026

Between October 5 and 18, four new pieces consider the difficult relationship between what can be recorded and what can be understood. They move from the protective value of leaving certain things undocumented to the selective remembrance preserved in medieval manuscripts, then to the coordination of incomplete knowledge and the difficulty of recognizing processes that unfold beyond administrative calendars.

The Library as a Community Sanctuary examines how libraries can reduce the pressures experienced by people in vulnerable circumstances through privacy, continuity, and institutional restraint, including decisions not to collect information or turn people's experiences into evidence of institutional success. Armenian Colophons and the Work of Remembrance explores the names, relationships, and circumstances preserved in medieval Armenian manuscripts, showing how these records commemorated certain contributors while leaving others outside the written account. No Bird Sees the Whole Sky draws on studies of homing pigeons to examine collective navigation, conflicting information, and unequal leadership, considering what these dynamics might suggest for collaborative searches across dispersed archives. Finally, From Quisquiza | Time begins with the uneven growth of young Andean trees to question how institutional reporting cycles and performance indicators can misrepresent processes whose development follows different timescales.


5Recently published

Recent books, articles, and other publications arising from my work on knowledge and memory, libraries and archives, music, and ecology.

El territorio como documento: Repensar los archivos desde una geoética de la memoria

Tabula Rasa · October 2026

A proposal to understand territory as a document and a living archive of ecological, historical, and cultural memory. The article develops the concept of a geoethics of memory and questions documentary practices that separate knowledge from its territorial contexts, proposing approaches to preservation grounded in responsibility toward the places, relationships, and communities that sustain that memory.

When Music Leaves No Objects: Yámana Vocal Practice and the Archival Bias Toward Instruments

Resonance · September 2026

An examination of how Yámana vocal practice exposes the object bias of archives, museums, and organological taxonomies. Focusing on a musical tradition centered on voice rather than instruments, the article develops a typology of absence and asks what preservation systems fail to collect, classify, or hear.

An Architecture of Vanishing: Toward a Protocol of Archival Disappearance

Journal of Contemporary Archival Studies · 2026

A proposal for treating disappearance, withdrawal, opacity, and finite duration as legitimate forms of archival care. The article develops temporal, spatial, relational, semantic, and material protocols through which archives may protect memory by allowing it to recede, transform, or end.

Fabricating Authenticity: Embera Nemboro Masks, Heritage Desire, and Metadata Fictions in Panama

International Journal of Heritage Studies · May 2026

An examination of how recently developed Embera basketry masks came to be presented as ancient ritual objects, and how markets, museums, and metadata can transform uncertain histories into apparently stable traditions.
